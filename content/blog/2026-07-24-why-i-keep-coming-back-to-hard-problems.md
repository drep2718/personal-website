There's a specific feeling I'm addicted to. You're staring at a problem that
looks impossible, the clock is running, and then — some structure you didn't
see a minute ago snaps into focus. Suddenly it's a graph, or a DP table, or a
greedy argument you can actually prove.

## The clock is the point

People assume the timer makes competitive programming stressful. For me it's
the opposite: the constraint is what forces clarity. You don't have time to
over-engineer. You find the *one* clean idea and you write it.

## What carries over

Most of what I love about it shows up everywhere else:

- Breaking a mess into invariants you can reason about
- Trusting the math before you trust the code
- Knowing when `O(n log n)` is hiding inside an `O(n^2)` problem

I get to teach a lot of this at Boiler Quant, and honestly explaining it is
half of why it sticks. If you can walk someone else through the *why*, you
understand it.
